# Spatial source transcriptions

The data in `data/models/spatial-models.js` is independent of WebGL. Geometry is generated by `spatialModelGeometry.js`; `SpatialModelStructure.js` draws only the cylinder and guide lines. Guide objects do not intercept picking.

## Stowe, 1989

Sources inspected directly:

- [Original I²R chart](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/Stowe_2.png).
- [Original explanation and 1989 copyright](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/Stowe_3.png).
- [Publication provenance and distinction from later revisions](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/pt_database.php?PT_id=38).

The poster contains **120 sites: 107 identifiable elements and 13 question marks**. The question marks occupy five remaining sites of the n=6 d ring, six sites of the n=7 p ring and two sites in the final s pair below the n=7 layer. They have no contemporary atomic-number mapping. Unq, Unp, Unh and Uns retain their printed historical symbols, while their detail links can use the present identities of elements 104–107.

The original explanation assigns the principal quantum number n to the level axis, m to the other in-plane axis, the sign of spin to the sides of the x axis, and l to the coloured rings. The reconstruction uses x = sign(spin) × (l − |m| + ½), depth = −m, and one horizontal layer per n. This coordinate construction reproduces the printed ring positions; it is a geometric interpretation of the original diagram, not a calculation of ground-state electron configurations. In particular, the source positions of La, Gd and Ac are deliberately retained. The final unlabelled s pair is the geometric continuation n=8; the number 8 itself is not printed in the reproduced poster.

Horizontal layer gaps are expanded according to their ring sizes, as in the source projection, to keep cells visible at the default camera angle. Those display distances do not encode an energy scale. The original s/p/d/f colours are translated into restrained pastel swatches. Decorative asterisks and halogen square marks are omitted because current detail content identifies chemical categories independently; element membership and quantum-coordinate positions are preserved.

## de Chancourtois, 1862

Sources inspected directly:

- [7 April 1862 Vis tellurique plate](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/chancourtois_1862_whole_a.png), reproduced courtesy of the Master and Fellows of St Catharine’s College, Cambridge, through Peter Wothers.
- [Reference collection, including warnings about later simplified versions](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/pt_database.php?PT_id=7).
- [Science Museum’s 1925 physical realization](https://collection.sciencemuseum.org.uk/objects/co13134/model-of-the-periodic-system-of-de-chancourtois-model), linked from [the museum-model catalogue entry](https://www.meta-synthesis.com/webbook/35_pt/pt_database.php?PT_id=1035). This later object informs cylindrical construction, not the transcribed membership. The museum host returned HTTP 403 to automated retrieval; its link and description are preserved by the reference collection.

The reconstructed reading contains **123 slots**, including **59 distinct mapped modern element identities**, **17 unmapped entries** and **six combined positions** where labels share a source characteristic number. It includes both the named catalogue entries and legible named comparison marks in the main graph. Repeated weights have separate slot identities; the duplicate marks at opposite edges of the unrolled cylinder are one physical seam position, not additional atoms.

Position is determined by the source characteristic number w: azimuth = 2πw/16 and height = (128 − w) × pitch/16. The cylinder includes all sixteen revolutions from 0 to 256. The source values are **not** modern atomic masses or atomic numbers. For example Fe is placed at 56, Ni at 59, Co at 60 and Au at 200. The vertical pitch is compressed relative to the original 45-degree construction so the complete object can fit a screen. The displayed edition explicitly identifies this adaptation.

Mixed entries and uncertain identities remain visible. Er at 58 means ethyl; Pr at 86 means propyl, whereas Pr at 199 means platinum. Br at 114 means butyl, distinct from bromine at 79. Didymium and ilmenium have no forced modern identity. The questioned erbium/terbium positions at 83–85 remain historical annotations rather than asserted modern elements. Co-located radicals and elements, including Si/Am at 36 and Ce/EA at 92, share one selectable historical slot.

This is an annotated spatial reading, not a facsimile of every mark on the plate. The source uses several combinations of underline, dots and coloured marker outlines; simple primes distinguish repeated positions in this reconstruction. The guide cylinder and pastel colours are new presentation choices. Small unnamed formula-only marks are not transcribed where their formula or referent cannot be read confidently: notably the catalogue/formula annotations around 142, 170, 176, 198, 226, 232 and 254. They are not replaced with guessed elements. The blank parts of the original weight range remain blank.

## Verification

`tests/spatial-models.test.mjs` checks all named Stowe elements and all unassigned sites, selected original quantum positions, unique slot IDs, the 16-weight-unit helix recurrence, repeated/compound identities, outward face picking and shared geometry resources. Browser integration additionally needs orbit/zoom, history/sidebar return and transitions into and out of these layouts.
